📖 What is teloa?

Most AI tools give you one assistant in one chat window. You ask a question, you get an answer, and then you start again from scratch. That works for quick questions, but it falls apart the moment you have a real project with more than one step.

teloa takes a different approach. Instead of one assistant, you build a team. You decide who is on that team and what each member is responsible for. One might be your researcher. Another might be your writer. A third might check the work before it comes back to you. Together, they handle the whole job, not just one message.

Think of it like hiring a small studio of people who never sleep, never forget the brief, and never mind being asked to try again. You stay in charge. You set the goal, you review the result, and you decide what happens next.

Everything runs on your own Windows computer. There is nothing to host, nothing to configure, and no technical knowledge required to get started.

✨ What you can do with it

  • Build a team that fits your work. Create as many AI team members as you need and give each one a clear role — planning, writing, checking, summarising, answering questions.

  • See the work as it happens. Watch your team move through a task step by step, so you always know where things stand and what is still left to do.
  • Keep everything in one place. Your teams, your tasks and your results live together in a single studio instead of being scattered across browser tabs.
  • Reuse what works. Once a team produces something you like, save it and bring it back the next time a similar job comes along.

🚀 Your first ten minutes with teloa

The first time you open teloa, you will see an empty studio waiting for you. Here is a comfortable way to begin.

  1. Name your team. Call it something that matches the work you have in mind — "Newsletter Team", "Research Crew", "Weekly Planning". A clear name makes everything easier to find later.
  2. Add your first team member. Give it a role in one sentence. For example: "You gather facts and summarise them clearly." That single line is all teloa needs.
  3. Add a second member. Try a reviewer whose job is to look over the first member's work and point out anything unclear. Two roles working together already feel very different from a single chat.
  4. Give the team a small task. Start with something you would normally spend fifteen minutes on — a short summary, a rough plan, a list of options.
  5. Read the result and adjust. If something is not quite right, tell the team what to change. Rewording your instruction is usually all it takes.

🧠 How it works, in plain terms

Every team member in teloa is made of two simple parts: a role description you write, and the AI model that does the thinking. When you send a task to your team, teloa passes the task to each member in turn, along with their role. Each member works on its part and hands the result along.

Because you write the roles yourself, you are effectively designing how your team behaves. A vague role gives vague work. A sharp, specific role gives sharp, specific work. That is the whole skill — and it is a skill anyone can learn in an afternoon.

teloa itself stays out of your way. It does not decide what your team should be, and it does not rewrite your instructions. It gives you a clean place to organise the work, and it keeps the pieces connected.

❓ Common questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Everything in teloa is written in normal sentences. If you can explain a job to a person, you can explain it to a team member.

How many team members should I create?

Start with two. Small teams are easier to follow and easier to fix when something goes wrong. Add more only when you can clearly name the extra job.

Can I use my team for more than one project?

Yes. Teams are saved, so you can return to one you have already built and reuse it whenever a similar task comes along.

Will my team do things without asking me?

No. You set the task, you see the work, and you decide what to keep. teloa is a studio you run, not a service that runs without you.

What if a result is not good enough?

Send it back with a note about what is wrong. Nine times out of ten the fastest fix is to make the role description more specific rather than to repeat the same request.

💡 Three habits that make teams work better

Give each member one job. A team member asked to research, write and fact-check at the same time will do all three a little badly. Split the jobs and the quality jumps.

Be specific about the output. "Write a summary" leaves a lot of room. "Write a five-bullet summary for someone who has never heard of this topic" leaves almost none — and gets you something you can use straight away.

Keep a team you trust. Once you have a team that reliably produces what you want, save it as your default and stop rebuilding it every week. That is where most of the time savings come from.
